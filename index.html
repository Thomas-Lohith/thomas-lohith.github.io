Projects

Featured case study · Industrial PhD, 2023 – present

Bridge health monitoring: from raw vibration to reviewed anomaly flags

Milano Serravalle – Milano Tangenziali · Università di Pavia · OWL University of Applied Sciences (inIT)

The problem

A highway operator instruments its bridges with 100+ triaxial accelerometers and inclinometers sampling at 100 Hz. At full rate that is up to ~2.7 billion readings a day (106 sensors × 3 axes × 100 Hz × 86,400 s). Most of it is ordinary traffic and ambient vibration. Engineers need early, trustworthy signals of abnormal behaviour, not terabytes of raw signal.

My role

I own the data path end to end: ingestion and storage, preprocessing and annotation, feature extraction, anomaly-detection models, and the KPIs and reports that the operator's engineers use to judge them.

Approach

  • Converted raw per-sensor CSV logs to columnar Parquet and processed them with Polars/Pandas so that multi-day, multi-sensor analysis fits on a workstation.
  • Designed event-based filtering and downsampling that keeps vehicle-induced and abnormal events at full resolution and stores quiet periods compactly (first-author paper, IEEE ETFA 2025).
  • Related vibration amplitude and spectral content to traffic volume, heavy-vehicle share and weigh-in-motion loads, separating normal load response from behaviour worth flagging.
  • Trained deep-learning and probabilistic models on multivariate time series and defined the KPIs used to evaluate detection performance.

Outcome

  • A reproducible pipeline from raw sensor files to anomaly flags and reports, used in an ongoing industrial collaboration.
  • Peer-reviewed publication on storage-efficient filtering of large sensor data (IEEE ETFA 2025).
  • Open-source analysis tooling (vibration, spectral and traffic analysis, threshold computation) under the MIT licence.

Detection metrics and site data are confidential to the infrastructure operator. I'm happy to walk through the methods and evaluation design in an interview.

About

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at the Università di Pavia, doing industrial doctoral research with Milano Serravalle – Milano Tangenziali, and currently a visiting researcher at OWL University of Applied Sciences in Lemgo, Germany. My work applies machine learning to large-scale sensor networks on highway bridges. In practice that means building data pipelines, engineering features from multivariate signals, training and validating models, and making them run reliably outside a notebook.

I came to ML from mechanical engineering and robotics: I programmed collaborative robots at a Milan start-up and did materials-testing research in India. That background means I'm comfortable on the shop floor and with the physics behind the data, as well as in the code. It's useful anywhere ML meets physical assets: manufacturing, energy, mobility and infrastructure.
